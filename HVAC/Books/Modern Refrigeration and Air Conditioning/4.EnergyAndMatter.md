# Systems and Measurement
- The base units of the US Customary measurement system include the foot (ft) for distance and the pound (lb) for mass
- The base SI units include the meter (m) for distance and the gram (g) for mass.
# Matter and Energy
- _**Matter**_ as the building blocks of the universe. Everything we can touch is made up of matter.
- Matter exists in three states: solid, liquid, and gas

- _**Energy**_ is the ability to effect a change in matter    
- It can also be defined as the ability or capacity to perform work.

- Using the building block analogy, you can think of matter as a pile of blocks. Energy is required to transform those building blocks from a loose pile into a wall.

- Energy comes in many different forms. It can be chemical, thermal, light, electrical, or mechanical
- Subdivided into potential and kinetic energy:
    - Potential energy is stored energy. Examples of potential energy are water behind a dam
    - Kinetic energy is energy doing work. Examples are water flowing over a dam
- The _**law of conservation of energy**_ states that energy cannot be created or destroyed; it is simply changed from one form to another

- Think of the building block analogy once again. Kinetic energy is used to stack the building blocks to create the wall. As the wall is created, potential energy is stored in each of the stacked bricks. The higher a block is lifted, the more kinetic energy is used to lift it and the more potential energy it stores when it is in position. If the wall topples over, the potential energy is converted back to kinetic energy as the blocks fall to the ground
# Mass and Weight
- The ==gravitational force exerted by the earth== on an object is expressed as the object’s _**weight**_.
	- object’s weight varies based on its distance from earth
- The term _**mass**_ is used to express that the quantity of material is the same, regardless of the change in the force of gravity.

- A small subscript f appearing under the pound unit abbreviation (lbf) indicates that the unit refers to the object’s weight (**==pounds of force==** or pound force).

# Density
- _**Density**_ is a substance’s mass per unit of volume.
- In the US Customary system, density is expressed as **pounds per cubic foot** (lb/ft3). In the SI system, density is expressed in **kilograms per cubic meter** (kg/m3).
## Specific Volume
- _**Specific volume**_ is the volume of a specific amount of gas under standard conditions
    - Standard conditions are 68°F (20°C) and 14.7 psia (101.3 kPa).
    - The specific volume is the inverse of the density. The higher a specific volume, the lower its density. The lower a specific volume, the higher the density.
- If a gas has a greater specific volume than air, the gas is called a light gas. If it occupies less space than air, it is classified as a heavy gas
```txt
1 lb/ft3 = 16 kg/m3 
1 kg/m3 = 0.0625 lb/ft3
```
## Specific Gravity (Relative Density)
- Specific gravity is the ratio of the mass of a certain volume of a liquid or a solid compared to the mass of an equal volume of water
    - Water is given a specific gravity of 1.
    - Objects that float on water have a specific gravity less than 1.
    - Objects that sink in water have a specific gravity greater than 1
- The relative density of gases is defined as the ratio of the mass of a certain volume of a gas as compared to the mass of an ==_**equal volume of hydrogen**_==
    - The mass of both gases are measured at 68°F and 29.92 in. Hg pressure
    - Since hydrogen is the lightest of all gases, all other gases will have relative densities greater than 1.
# Force, Work and Power
## Force
**Force (F)** is energy applied to matter that, unless counteracted by opposing forces, causes a change in the matter’s velocity.
The unit of force is the pound force (lbf).
